# Quantum Coherence
> The ability of quantum system to maintain a stable, connected phase relationship between multiple states.   
> It is the core property that allows qubits to exist in a superposition and entangle with one another.   [QuEra_Coherence](https://www.quera.com/glossary/coherence)

- Key aspects of coherence
  - Superposition: a qubit in a combination of 0 and 1 (linear combination)
  - Interference: combine quantum states constructively or destructively to guide (preferred) probabilities
  - Fragility: disrupts easily when interact with environment

### Coherence Time (lifespan)
Determines how long a qubit can hold its data before fading.

### Decoherence
When the system loses coherence due to interactions with environment, and turns back to a classical bit.

### Coherent evolution (the action)
It describes the way a quantum system changes over time while keeping its quantum properties intact. 
- full coherent evolution: a quantum system changes from its start state to its end state with no leaks, noise, or data loss.

Summary:  
Coherence is what qubits hold data about itself and its entangled qubit.  
Superposition is the qubit itself can be in either 0 or 1 or both state.  
Entanglement is multiple qubits have connections with each other (usually pairs).   
Due to noise, heat etc, qubits loss those data during the process, which is called decoherence.   
The total time until qubits start losing data is coherent time.
