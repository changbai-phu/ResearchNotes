# Title: Additivity of quantum capacities in simple non-degradable quantum channels
Authors: Graeme Smith and Peixue Wu   
Year:  2025      
Institute:     
Link: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.03927     
Document: [2025-Smith&Wu-Additivity-Channel-Capacity-Non-degradable](resources/papers_download/QComm_2025-GraemeSmith-Additivity_quantum_capa_non_degradable.pdf)   
  
This paper addresses a long-standing challenge in quantum communication: calculating the exact information-carrying capacity of noisy quantum channels


## One-sentence summary
The authors found a way to mathematically calculate the exact capacity of certain non-degradable channels (which mirror real-world noise better) by proving that their coherent information remains additive under weaker, more flexible structural conditions.

*Notes*:
- n this paper, we introduce two families of non-degradable channels whose coherent information remains additive, making their quantum capacities tractable. -- Abstract

## Notes-taken
| Model / Concept | What it means physically |
|---|---|
| "Outperforming" the Environment (Weaker than Degradability) | Bob doesn't need to hold a strictly "degradable" version of Eve's state. He just needs to consistently receive more useful quantum correlations than the environment gets, under relaxed conditions. |
| Additivity without "Outperforming" | A constructed channel family where Bob does not outperform the environment, yet structural constraints still prevent superadditivity, keeping calculations tractable. |
| Weak vs. Strong Additivity | **Weak Additivity:** Sending $n$ identical copies of this specific channel in parallel yields no superadditive boost ($Q^{(1)}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n}) = n Q^{(1)}(\mathcal{N})$).<br><br>**Strong Additivity:** Combining this channel with any other arbitrary channel $\mathcal{M}$ still yields no superadditive boost ($Q^{(1)}(\mathcal{N} \otimes \mathcal{M}) = Q^{(1)}(\mathcal{N}) + Q^{(1)}(\mathcal{M})$). |
